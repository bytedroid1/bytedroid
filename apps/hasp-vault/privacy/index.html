Applies to the Hasp Vault apps for iOS and Android (com.hifazat.vault).

The short version. Your documents, passwords, cards and notes are stored on your phone and are never sent to us. We do not have a server that holds your data, we do not have accounts, and we cannot see anything in your vault. The app does send a small amount of technical information — crash reports, basic usage statistics, and your purchase status — which is described in section 3. None of it contains anything from inside your vault.

If you turn on Sync (Hasp Pro, version 1.3.0 and later), an encrypted copy of your vault is kept in your own iCloud or Google Drive so your phones stay the same. It is locked on your phone before it is uploaded, and it never reaches us.

1. Who we are

Hasp Vault is an offline personal vault app. In this policy, “we” means the developer of Hasp Vault and “you” means the person using the app. Contact details are in section 11.

2. What stays on your device

Everything you put into the vault is stored locally in the app’s private storage, which other apps on your phone cannot read:

  • Passwords, bank cards, notes and document details — held in a database encrypted with AES-256. The encryption key is generated on your device and kept in the iOS Keychain or the Android Keystore.
  • Document files (scans, photos, PDFs) — stored in the app’s private storage area.
  • Your PIN — never stored. We keep only a salted PBKDF2-SHA256 hash, which cannot be turned back into your PIN.
  • Break-in photos, if you switch that feature on — taken by the front camera after three wrong PIN attempts and saved into the same encrypted database. They stay on your phone.

None of the above is ever transmitted to us. It leaves your phone only in the ways described in section 3, each of which you start yourself. We have no ability to access, recover or reset it.

3. What leaves your device, and when

Crash reports and usage statistics

The app uses Google Firebase Crashlytics and Firebase Analytics. Crashlytics sends a report when the app crashes: the error, where in the code it happened, your device model, operating system version and app version. Analytics records which screens are opened and how often the app is used.

To count users without knowing who they are, both use random identifiers created on your device — an install ID, and the vendor identifier Apple gives each app — which are not linked to your name or to anything in your vault. Google also estimates your approximate location, such as country and city, from your IP address. The app itself never uses your phone’s GPS or location services.

Neither is given anything from inside your vault. Document names, passwords, card numbers, note text and file contents are never included — the app is built so that this data cannot reach them. Google’s handling of it is covered by the Firebase privacy policy.

Purchases

If you buy Hasp Vault Pro, the purchase is handled by Google Play or the Apple App Store, depending on where you installed the app, and recorded by RevenueCat, which we use to check whether your purchase is active. RevenueCat receives the purchase itself and an anonymous identifier it generates — not your name, your email or anything from your vault. See the RevenueCat privacy policy.

Advertising — none

There is no advertising in Hasp Vault and no advertising SDK in the app. It does not read your device’s advertising identifier: on Android the permissions that would allow it are removed at build time, and on iPhone the app does not include Apple’s advertising framework.

Backups — only when you create one

Exporting a backup produces a single encrypted .avault file, protected by a passphrase you choose. You decide where it goes. It is never uploaded anywhere by the app. We cannot open it, and neither can anyone without your passphrase — so if you forget it, that backup cannot be recovered.

Phone-to-phone transfer — only when you start one

This feature sends your vault directly to another handset across your own Wi-Fi or hotspot. The data is encrypted before it leaves, the receiving phone is reached by its local network address only, and nothing passes through any server of ours or anyone else’s. The transfer window closes after one delivery or three minutes, whichever comes first.

Sync — only if you turn it on

Sync (Hasp Pro, version 1.3.0 and later) keeps the same vault on your phones. It is off until you switch it on in Settings → Sync, and it uses your own cloud storage — never a server of ours:

  • iCloud (iPhone and iPad) — the vault is stored in Hasp Vault’s own private space in your iCloud account. The key that opens it is kept in your iCloud Keychain, which Apple encrypts end to end.
  • Google Drive (Android, and iPhone if you choose it) — you sign in with your Google account, and the app asks for one permission: its own hidden app folder in your Drive. It cannot see, open or change any of your other files in Drive. The key that opens the vault is kept in that same hidden folder.

Everything is encrypted on your phone with AES-256-GCM before it is uploaded, so Apple or Google store only locked files. Because the key travels with your account, the copy in the cloud is as safe as that Apple or Google account — protect it with a strong password and two-step verification.

Google account information — Sync with Google Drive only

When you sign in with Google to use Sync, Google shares your name, email address and profile picture with the app, and gives it access to the app’s own folder in your Google Drive (the drive.appdata permission). The app uses your email address only to show which account Sync uses, and keeps it on your phone; it does not use your name or picture. Through the Drive permission it reads and writes only the encrypted vault files it created itself.

This information is never sent to us, sold, used for advertising, shared with anyone, or read by a person. Hasp Vault’s use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. You can remove the app’s access at any time in your Google Account, under Security → Your connections to third-party apps & services.

4. What we never collect

  • No Hasp Vault account or sign-up, and no phone number. If you use Sync with Google Drive, your Google email address stays on your phone (section 3) — it is never sent to us.
  • No copies of your documents, passwords, cards or notes — ever.
  • No contacts, no calendar, and no precise location — the app never uses GPS or location services.
  • No advertising identifier.

6. Children

Hasp Vault is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect information from them.

7. How long your data is kept

On your phone, for as long as you keep the app installed. If Sync is on, the encrypted copy in your iCloud or Google Drive stays there until you delete it (section 8). There is never a copy on a server of ours.

Without Sync, uninstalling the app permanently deletes your vault. There is no recovery code and no way for us to restore it. If you are changing phones, export a backup first — or turn on Sync, and your vault comes back when you turn it on again on the new phone.

8. Your choices

  • Delete everything — Settings → Erase vault destroys all content and the encryption keys, returning the app to a fresh state. Uninstalling has the same effect.
  • Turn off expiry reminders — remove the expiry date from a document, or switch notifications off for the app in your phone’s system settings.
  • Turn off Sync — Settings → Sync → Turn off sync. The phone stops syncing; what is already in your cloud is left as it is.
  • Delete the copy in your cloud — iCloud: in the Settings app, tap your name → iCloud → Manage Storage → Hasp Vault → Delete Data. Google Drive: on drive.google.com, open Settings → Manage apps → Hasp Vault → Options → Delete hidden app data.

Because your data never reaches us, requests to access, correct or delete it are things only you can carry out — from inside the app, or in your own iCloud or Google account.

9. Security

The vault database is encrypted with AES-256 using a key held in the iOS Keychain or the Android Keystore. Your PIN is stored only as a salted PBKDF2-SHA256 hash, and repeated wrong attempts trigger increasing lockout delays. The app hides its contents in the app switcher and locks itself after a period of inactivity. On Android it also blocks screenshots while unlocked; iOS does not allow an app to block screenshots, so that protection is Android-only.

Files that Sync uploads are encrypted on your phone with AES-256-GCM, each sealed to its own role, so a file in the cloud cannot be read, changed or swapped for another without the key.

No system is perfect, and a phone that is compromised at the operating-system level cannot be protected by any app. Keep your device updated and your screen lock enabled.

10. Changes to this policy
